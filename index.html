The category

What is advised trading?

Two things have existed for years, in separate places. Advised trading is what happens when you put them together — and until Finnovest, nobody had.

Already existed

Professional advice, in banks

Licensed advisors giving real guidance to real people. Personal, considered, and built on genuine expertise — but delivered one conversation at a time, which is why it has always reached the largest accounts and few others.

Already existed

Trading, in apps

Fast, cheap, independent trading apps that put the whole market in a pocket. Excellent at execution, and completely silent on what to do. The trader is on their own.

Did not exist

Advised trading — professional human advice, delivered personally, and executable in a single tap.

Why "executable" is the hard word

The orders depend on each other.

A recommendation is rarely one trade. Buying usually means selling something first — and the sale has to complete before the purchase can be funded. Everywhere else, that is four separate actions and a phone call.

Why it took this long

Personal advice never scaled, so nobody offered it.

A trading app is not obliged to give advice. But the moment it claims to give personal advice, that advice has to genuinely be personal — matched to what each client holds, what they can afford, and what they are trying to achieve. Done by hand, that is one advisor and one client at a time, which does not work at retail scale.

So brokerages stayed out of it. Their clients did not stop wanting guidance — they took it from finfluencers, forums and general market calls, none of which has ever seen a single one of their portfolios.

One advisor. Endless traders. Each one receiving something different.

Platform

From one recommendation to ten thousand different ones.

The platform follows the client, not the architecture. Here is what happens from the moment someone opens an account to the moment their orders fill.

1 · Onboarding

The client arrives with a portfolio or without one. An existing portfolio is imported as it stands, whatever the starting point. A client with no portfolio has one built from the advisor's recommendations, shaped by their own profile — and approves it before anything executes.

2 · Constraints

Risk profile, goals, personal information, current holdings, and available cash and credit. This is the set that makes one recommendation land differently for every client, and it is applied to everything that follows.

3 · The house view

The head of advisory sets the institution's position on the market — the trading vision the desk is working to.

4 · The advisor

Each advisor turns that view into a recommendation for their own book: a specific thesis, a purchase, the funding strategy behind it, and the reasoning written in their own words. Two layers of professional judgement before any software runs.

5 · Translation

The Generic Constraints Algorithm resolves that one instruction against every portfolio: which holding funds the purchase here, how much this client can take on, and whether the recommendation suits them at all. If it does not, it is never sent.

6 · Execution

The client sees the thesis, the reasoning behind each individual order, and how long the recommendation stays open. They can simulate the effect on their portfolio before deciding, and read the full documentation. One press approves everything — the Automatic Order Management Module sequences the interdependent orders, with no human intervention.

7 · Follow-up

Sent, opened, executed — per client for the advisor to act on, and in aggregate for the institution to learn from.

Stage five in detail

What "personal" actually requires.

A recommendation to buy is meaningless until you know what this particular client can sell, afford, and carry. Five things determine it.

Existing holdings

What they already own decides what can be trimmed to fund a purchase — and whether they are already overweight in the thing being recommended.

Cash available

Whether the purchase needs funding at all, or whether it can be made outright.

Credit available

What the account permits beyond cash, and what that means for sizing.

Risk profile

Whether this instrument belongs in this portfolio, and at what weight. Where it does not fit, the recommendation is filtered out entirely.

Personal goals

What the client is investing towards, and the horizon they are working to.

All of it, simultaneously

Across the whole book at once. That is the part the Israel Innovation Authority recognised as a breakthrough.

The challenge

A brokerage competing in a market where the obvious lever is price, and where personal investment advice had always belonged to the banks. Its clients traded independently, with no professional guidance attached to the screen they were already looking at — every decision about new money, a position to trim, or a portfolio drifting off its risk level fell to them alone.

What they launched

Next Trade, live August 2026 — built on Finnovest and delivered inside the Phoenix Investment House app the client already uses. Excellence's investment experts issue a recommendation; the platform resolves it against each client's own holdings and stated risk level, then pushes it to their phone with the trade instruction already prepared. The client approves or declines, and only on approval does the order route to the exchange. Offered at no additional cost to clients with portfolios from ₪20,000, after a six-question profile and a digital agreement. More than a year in development.

Why it matters

The first time independent traders received personal, executable recommendations inside the app they already traded in. Not a model portfolio and not a general market insight, but a personal recommendation created by a professional, authorised investment advisor. The platform resolves each recommendation against the client's own position and lets investors stay in control — approve or decline. It also allows new investors to build a portfolio based on the advisor's recommendations, shaped by their own profile.

For retail investors

The professionals' thinking. Your portfolio. Your call.

If you trade on your own, you have probably taken advice from somewhere — a finfluencer, a forum, a general market call. None of them has ever seen your portfolio. Advised trading is different: a licensed professional at your broker decides, and the platform works out what that means for what you actually hold.

Step one

A professional committee forms a view

At your bank or brokerage, licensed professionals research the market and reach a decision — reduce exposure to a sector, add a position, rebalance. The same analysis that has always driven institutional money.

Step two

It's translated to your portfolio

The platform reads what you actually hold, your risk level and what you're trying to achieve, then works out what that view means for you specifically — which holdings, how much, and whether it suits you at all. If it doesn't fit you, it never arrives.

Step three

You approve, or you don't

It reaches you with the reasoning in plain language. Approve it, ask a question, or leave it. Saying no is a normal answer, and nothing moves unless you tap.

Why this was out of reach

Professional advice used to be a question of portfolio size.

An advisor could only speak to one client at a time, so the most attentive service went to the largest accounts. Everyone else got a newsletter, a generic model, or nothing. The constraint was never the quality of the thinking — it was how many people one professional could reach in a day.

That constraint is what the technology removes. The same committee view now reaches thousands of investors at once, individually sized for each one, without anyone having to wait their turn.

The advice is professional. The fit is personal. The decision stays yours.

You've arrived here from a badge inside your bank's or broker's app. Here's the short version of who we are and what our involvement means for your money.

We are a technology company

Finnovest builds the software your institution uses to send you personalised recommendations. We're not a bank, a broker, or an advisor.

We never hold your money

Your assets stay exactly where they are, with your institution. We have no custody of anything and never will.

Your advisor writes the advice

Every recommendation you see originates with a licensed professional at your institution. Our software personalises and delivers it — it doesn't decide it.

Nothing happens until you tap

No order is placed without your explicit approval. That's what non-discretionary means, and it's not optional.
