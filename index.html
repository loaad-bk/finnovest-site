Who is advising you

A licensed advisor. A real person, accountable to you and to a regulator.

The advice comes from a licensed professional at your bank or brokerage. Someone who reads the market every day, forms a view, and writes down the reasoning before it reaches you. Regulated, accountable, and paid to get it right. That advice arrives already fitted to what you actually hold, and it waits for your tap. Not a finfluencer's call to a million followers, not a formula sorting you into a bucket, not a manager trading your money without asking. A professional, thinking about you.

And if a recommendation does not suit your profile, you never see it. Every one is checked for suitability against your own account before it is sent.

Until now this kind of advice was reserved for the wealthy few, because it was delivered one conversation at a time. Advised-trading brings it to every retail investor.

One tap, multiple orders

One tap executes the whole recommendation.

A recommendation is rarely one trade. Buying usually means selling something first, and the sale has to complete before the purchase can be funded. On Finnovest you approve once. The platform sells, confirms the cash, buys and reconciles, in the right order, with no human intervention.

The translation process

What "personalization" actually requires.

A recommendation to buy is meaningless until you know what this particular client can sell, afford, and carry. Five things determine it.

Existing holdings

What they already own decides what can be trimmed to fund a purchase, and whether they are already overweight in the thing being recommended.

Cash available

Whether the purchase needs funding at all, or whether it can be made outright.

Credit available

What the account permits beyond cash, and what that means for sizing.

Risk profile

Whether this instrument belongs in this portfolio, and at what weight. Where it does not fit, the recommendation is filtered out entirely.

Personal goals

What the client is investing towards, and the horizon they are working to.

All of it, simultaneously

Across the whole book at once. That is the part the Israel Innovation Authority recognised as a breakthrough.

The technology

Two components that make advised trading possible at scale.

One creates the recommendations, the other executes them. 

Generic Constraints Algorithm

Handles any operational or client-specific constraint, automating the personal suitability process for a large number of clients simultaneously. The result is a recommendation tailored to each individual client, despite wide variation between one portfolio and the next.

Automatic Order Management Module

Manages and supervises the sending of multiple interdependent trade orders for each individual client, with no human intervention, executing several trades from a single press of the approval button. Interdependent is the hard part: when a sale funds a purchase, the orders rely on each other.

Compliance, built in

Suitability checked before it is sent. Documented before anyone asks.

Every recommendation is verified against each client's risk profile, holdings, cash and credit before it reaches them. Where it does not fit, it is filtered out, not softened. The reasoning, the checks and the fills are recorded as they happen, so the file is already written when the supervisor wants it. Personalised advice at retail scale, with no exception queue at the end of the day.

01

Excellence Trade · Phoenix Investment House

Next Trade · the first advised-trading launch
The challenge

A brokerage competing in a market where the obvious lever is price, and where personal investment advice had always belonged to the banks. Its clients traded independently, with no professional guidance attached to the screen they were already looking at: every decision about new money, a position to trim, or a portfolio drifting off its risk level fell to them alone.

What they launched

Next Trade, live August 2026, built on Finnovest and delivered inside the Phoenix Investment House app the client already uses. Excellence's investment experts issue a recommendation; the platform resolves it against each client's own holdings and stated risk level, then pushes it to their phone with the trade instruction already prepared. The client approves or declines, and only on approval does the order route to the exchange. Offered at no additional cost to clients with portfolios from ₪20,000, after a six-question profile and a digital agreement. More than a year in development.

Why it matters

The first time independent traders received personal, executable recommendations inside the app they already traded in. Not a model portfolio and not a general market insight, but a personal recommendation created by a professional, authorised investment advisor. The platform resolves each recommendation against the client's own position and lets retail investors stay in control: approve or decline. It also allows new investors to build a portfolio based on the advisor's recommendations, shaped by their own profile.

The challenge

A large retail book and an advisory desk that could only reach clients one conversation at a time. The digital channel carried plenty of information, but nothing a client could act on.

For retail investors

The professionals' thinking. Your portfolio. Your call.

If you trade on your own, you have probably taken advice from somewhere: a finfluencer, a forum, a general market call. None of them has ever seen your portfolio. Advised-trading is different: a licensed professional at your broker advises, the platform works out what that means for what you actually hold, and you decide.

Step one

A professional committee forms a view

At your bank or brokerage, licensed professionals research the market and form a recommendation: reduce exposure to a sector, add a position, rebalance. The same analysis that has always driven institutional money.

Step two

It's translated to your portfolio

The platform reads what you actually hold, your risk level and what you're trying to achieve, then works out what that view means for you specifically: which holdings, how much, and whether it suits you at all. If it doesn't fit you, it never arrives.

Step three

You approve, or you don't

It reaches you with the reasoning in plain language. Approve it, ask a question, or leave it. Saying no is a normal answer, and nothing moves unless you tap.

Why this was out of reach

Professional advice used to be a question of portfolio size.

An advisor could only speak to one client at a time, so the most attentive service went to the largest accounts. Everyone else got a newsletter, a generic model, or nothing. The constraint was never the quality of the thinking. It was how many people one professional could reach in a day.

That constraint is what the technology removes. The same committee view now reaches thousands of retail investors at once, individually sized for each one, without anyone having to wait their turn.

The advice is professional. The fit is personal. The decision stays yours.
