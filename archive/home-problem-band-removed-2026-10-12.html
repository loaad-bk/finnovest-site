The decision layer is moving outside the institution

Retail investors consume advice differently.

At the moment that matters most, when making the investment decision, retail investors are often on their own. They turn to social media, online communities, financial platforms and AI tools for ideas and guidance, but most of what they find is generic and disconnected from their portfolio, risk, goals and constraints. The demand for advice is still there. What has changed is how they want to consume it.

These investors are also far more active than traditional advisory models typically assume. They want to react faster, make more frequent decisions and receive ideas across a broader range of asset classes, including individual stocks.


Retail investors don’t want less advice. They want advice built for the way they trade.

More personal, more dynamic and more actionable, while remaining professional, responsible and fully compliant.

What it means for your institution

Your institution may hold the account. Someone else may own the client relationship.

When the advice layer moves outside your institution, influence over client decisions and asset flows moves with it. Whoever owns that layer increasingly influences where the money goes, leaving your institution at risk of becoming execution infrastructure.
