Step by step

From account opening to orders filled.

1 · Onboarding

The client arrives with a portfolio or without one. An existing portfolio is imported as it stands, whatever the starting point. A client with no portfolio has one built from the advisor's recommendations, shaped by their own profile and approves it before anything executes.

2 · Constraints

Risk profile, goals, personal information, current holdings, and available cash and credit. This is the set that makes one recommendation land differently for every client, and it is applied to everything that follows.

3 · The house view

The head of advisory sets the institution's position on the market and the trading vision of the financial house.

4 · The advisor

Each advisor turns the 'house view' into a recommendation for their own book: a specific thesis, a purchase, the funding strategy behind it, and the reasoning written in their own words. Two layers of professional judgement before any software runs.

5 · translation

Finnovest Generic Constraints Algorithm resolves that one instruction against every portfolio: which holding funds the purchase here, how much this client can take on, and whether the recommendation suits them at all. If it does not, it is never sent. What that requires ↓

6 · Execution

The client sees the thesis, the reasoning behind each individual order, and how long the recommendation stays open. They can simulate the effect on their portfolio before deciding, and read the full documentation. One press approves everything (bundeled set of sell and buy orders) the Automatic Order Management Module sequences the interdependent orders, with no human intervention.

7 · Follow-up

Sent, opened, executed: per client for the advisor to act on, and in aggregate for the institution to learn from.

The translation process

What "personalization" actually requires.

A recommendation to buy is meaningless until you know what this particular client can sell, afford, and carry. Five things determine it.

Existing holdings

What they already own decides what can be trimmed to fund a purchase, and whether they are already overweight in the thing being recommended.

Cash available

Whether the purchase needs funding at all, or whether it can be made outright.

Credit available

What the account permits beyond cash, and what that means for sizing.

Risk profile

Whether this instrument belongs in this portfolio, and at what weight. Where it does not fit, the recommendation is filtered out entirely.

Personal goals

What the client is investing towards, and the horizon they are working to.

All of it, simultaneously

Across the whole book at once. That is the part the Israel Innovation Authority recognised as a breakthrough.

The technology

Two components that make advised-trading possible at scale.

One creates the recommendations, the other executes them. 

Generic Constraints Algorithm

Handles any operational or client-specific constraint, automating the personal suitability process for a large number of clients simultaneously. The result is a recommendation tailored to each individual client, despite wide variation between one portfolio and the next.

Automatic Order Management Module

Manages and supervises the sending of multiple interdependent trade orders for each individual client, with no human intervention, executing several trades from a single press of the approval button. Interdependent is the hard part: when a sale funds a purchase, the orders rely on each other.

Compliance, built in

Suitability checked before it is sent. Documented before anyone asks.

Every recommendation is verified against each client's risk profile, holdings, cash and credit before it reaches them. Where it does not fit, it is filtered out, not softened. The reasoning, the checks and the fills are recorded as they happen, so the file is already written when the supervisor wants it. Personalised advice at retail scale, with no exception queue at the end of the day.
